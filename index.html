Pink Floyd

Pink Floyd is a British rock band, founded in London in 1965. It is considered a cultural icon of the 20th century and one of the most influential, successful, and acclaimed bands in the history of popular music. It gained great popularity within the underground scene thanks to its psychedelic and spacey music, which, over time, evolved into progressive rock and symphonic rock, acquiring the popularity for which it is remembered today. It is known for its songs with high philosophical content, along with sonic experimentation, innovative album covers, and elaborate live shows. Its sales exceed 280 million albums sold worldwide, 97.5 million of them in the United States alone, making it one of the best-selling bands in history. Initially, the group consisted of drummer Nick Mason, keyboardist and vocalist Richard Wright, bassist and vocalist Roger Waters, and guitarist and lead vocalist Syd Barrett, who became the band's first frontman after the brief stint of Bob Klose, who never appeared on any albums. Barrett's erratic and unpredictable behavior, caused by excessive drug use (especially LSD), led to his friend David Gilmour joining the group in December 1967. A few months later, Barrett left, and the quartet of Mason, Wright, Waters, and Gilmour became the definitive lineup.
